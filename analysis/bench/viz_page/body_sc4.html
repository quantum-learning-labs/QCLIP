Readout cost figures

How accuracy, gate count, shot cost and energy per decision scale with circuit size, for the post-selection (PS) model and two discard models. All trained models have 2 ansatz layers; the energy and time figures (3, 5–11) add untrained circuits of the same structure at 2, 4 and 8 layers, up to 124 qubits. Size grows along two axes: more output qubits e (1 qubit per noun wire), or more qubits per noun wire n (3 output qubits).

Figure 1

Qubits against accuracy

x = total qubits of the sentence circuit (output + all wires + auxiliaries, mean over sentences; = e + 3n + 1 for a typical sentence). Each model size is one point; sizes reached by growing the output register and by growing the qubits per wire sit on the same line. Where both reach the same size (10 qubits: e = 6, n = 1 and e = 3, n = 2), the line keeps the output-register model; the other is listed in the table only. Exact = unlimited shots; otherwise the expected accuracy at a fixed number of shots per decision. Points are means over 3 seeds, bars ±1 standard error.

Figure 5

QPU against the best classical method

Energy per discard decision (the readout a QPU needs fewest shots for), against total qubits, at a fixed circuit depth. QPU: shots = N0 / F2, where F is the factor by which device noise shrinks the decision margin, fitted to our Kingston noise runs (ln F = −0.187 − εeff × native two-qubit gates, εeff = 3.3×10−3); gate counts and energy from the transpiled compute-uncompute circuits, the circuits the noise fit was measured on. The output register e grows with the circuit (3, 6 or 8 qubits; see the table). Classical: every method we measured on an NVIDIA L4 (the most energy-efficient GPU available to us, which favours the classical side); the frontier is the cheapest at each size. Filled points are measured; hollow points are tensor-network contraction costs from the best path found, divided by the measured L4 efficiency. Path costs are upper bounds: a better contraction order can only lower them. One-off costs are included: finding a contraction path (classical) and transpiling to the device (QPU) are paid once per circuit shape and shared by the decisions that reuse that shape; at each reuse level the classical side uses whichever path is cheapest in total, so a path that took hours to find only counts where it is reused enough to pay for itself (Figure 8). The approximation target is the relative error of the decision margin allowed to every sampled or approximate method: QPU and sampler shots scale as (10% / target)² (N₀ gives 10%), and MPS must reach it against the exact result.

Figure 6

Error against energy at one circuit size

Relative error of the decision margin against energy per decision, for one circuit. The QPU and the classical sampler estimate the same quantity from samples: the sampler draws noiseless samples at the cost of one tensor-network amplitude each (a lower bound on its true cost, so it favours the classical side), the QPU needs 1/F2 times more shots. MPS points are bond dimensions 2–256, shown only where the exact result was computable to check them (deep circuits whose exact contraction is too wide have no MPS points: at 8 layers and 31 qubits, where the post-selection network could be checked, MPS was still 61% off at bond dimension 256). Calibration: N0 noiseless samples give a 10% margin error.

Where a QPU would win

Each cell: QPU (IBM Kingston model) energy divided by the best classical energy, as a power of ten, at that depth and size; below it the two-qubit error ε* at which the QPU would break even. Green cells: the QPU already wins at the chosen error. The error slider and the reuse level (one-off path-search and transpile costs) are shared with Figure 5.

Figure 8

One-off costs: path search and transpilation

Energy per decision against the number of decisions that share one circuit shape, for one circuit. Each tensor-network line uses the paths of one search method: its one-off search energy (search time × CPU power) is divided by the reuse and added to the contraction energy, so a cheap search with an expensive path is flat, and an expensive search with a cheap path falls with reuse. The QPU line adds the one-off Kingston transpile the same way. The frontier is the cheapest classical choice at each reuse.

Time per decision against qubits

Wall-clock time per discard decision against total qubits, for the same methods, models and settings as Figure 9 (its controls and these are linked). Classical: measured seconds on one NVIDIA L4, or the best path's FLOP count divided by the L4's measured rate (1.3×10¹² FLOP/s, median of the compute-bound runs). QPU: shots one after another on one device plus the job overhead, without queueing. Dotted thin lines are the one-off steps themselves, contraction-path search and transpilation, which are paid once per circuit shape; the per-decision curves include them divided by the reuse when they are counted. Guide lines mark a minute, an hour, a day, a year and the age of the universe.

Figure 11

How long to search for a contraction path

For one circuit: time per decision against the time spent searching for its contraction path. Dots are the paths found so far, each at its own search time (greedy, random-greedy, cotengra with 30 s–10 min budgets, a few longer runs); a long cotengra search per circuit, scored at 1 s, 3 s, 10 s, … up to 30 minutes (below 64 qubits) or 2 hours, adds the continuous curve as it lands from the cluster. Each line adds the search time, shared by the decisions that reuse the circuit shape, to the contraction time of the best path found so far, for the cheaper classical method (exact tensor network or the sampler); its lowest point (ring) is the best time to stop searching. The dashed line is contraction alone (unlimited reuse); the star is the provably optimal path where the exact search finished.

Against the true optimum, where it is computable

Sweep sc4: 10 model sizes × {PS, plain discard, warm-start discard} × 3 seeds, 2 text layers. Data: results/shotcost/viz/viz_sc4.json (branch shotcost, quantum-learning-labs/QCLIP), built by analysis/bench/viz_sc4.py from exact branch evaluations, noise runs, gate counts and device statistics.
Energy constants: IBM 25 kW, 250 µs repetition delay, 2 s job overhead over 190 circuits (public figures, assumed); IonQ 5 kW, 1.5 ms per shot, 10 s per circuit, gates in series (Knitter et al. 2026, arXiv 2605.02798). GPU scaling probe: 20-layer brickwork statevector on an RTX 3090 Ti. CLIP ViT-B/32: 0.36 J per decision. One-off costs: path search and transpilation timed as one process on a cluster CPU node, charged at 20 W (one busy core, assumed: the nodes expose no power counter); they scale linearly with that figure.
