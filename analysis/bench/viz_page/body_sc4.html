Readout cost figures

How accuracy, gate count, shot cost and energy per decision scale with circuit size, for the post-selection (PS) model and two discard models. All models have 2 ansatz layers. Size grows along two axes: more output qubits e (1 qubit per noun wire), or more qubits per noun wire n (3 output qubits).

Qubits against accuracy

x = total qubits of the sentence circuit (output + all wires + auxiliaries, mean over sentences; = e + 3n + 1 for a typical sentence). Each model size is one point; sizes reached by growing the output register and by growing the qubits per wire sit on the same line. Exact = unlimited shots; otherwise the expected accuracy at a fixed number of shots per decision. Points are means over 3 seeds, bars ±1 standard error.

Figure 5

QPU against the best classical method

Energy per discard decision (the readout a QPU needs fewest shots for), against total qubits, at a fixed circuit depth. QPU: shots = N0 / F2, where F is the factor by which device noise shrinks the decision margin, fitted to our Kingston noise runs (ln F = −0.187 − εeff × native two-qubit gates, εeff = 3.3×10−3); energy from the transpiled circuit duration. Classical: every method we measured on an NVIDIA L4 (the most energy-efficient GPU available to us, which favours the classical side); the frontier is the cheapest at each size. Filled points are measured; hollow points are tensor-network contraction costs from the best path found, divided by the measured L4 efficiency.

Figure 6

Error against energy at one circuit size

Relative error of the decision margin against energy per decision, for one circuit. The QPU and the classical sampler estimate the same quantity from samples: the sampler draws noiseless samples at the cost of one tensor-network amplitude each (a lower bound on its true cost, so it favours the classical side), the QPU needs 1/F2 times more shots. MPS points are bond dimensions 2–256, shown only where the exact result was computable to check them (deep circuits whose exact contraction is too wide have no MPS points: at 8 layers and 31 qubits, where the post-selection network could be checked, MPS was still 61% off at bond dimension 256). Calibration: N0 noiseless samples give a 10% margin error.

Where a QPU would win

Each cell: QPU (IBM Kingston model) energy divided by the best classical energy, as a power of ten, at that depth and size; below it the two-qubit error ε* at which the QPU would break even. Green cells: the QPU already wins at the chosen error. The slider is shared with Figure 5.

Sweep sc4: 10 model sizes × {PS, plain discard, warm-start discard} × 3 seeds, 2 text layers. Data: results/shotcost/viz/viz_sc4.json (branch shotcost, quantum-learning-labs/QCLIP), built by analysis/bench/viz_sc4.py from exact branch evaluations, noise runs, gate counts and device statistics.
Energy constants: IBM 25 kW, 250 µs repetition delay, 2 s job overhead over 190 circuits (public figures, assumed); IonQ 5 kW, 1.5 ms per shot, 10 s per circuit, gates in series (Knitter et al. 2026, arXiv 2605.02798). GPU scaling probe: 20-layer brickwork statevector on an RTX 3090 Ti. CLIP ViT-B/32: 0.36 J per decision.
