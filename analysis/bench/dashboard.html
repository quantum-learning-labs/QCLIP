With and without debiasing

IonQ's debiasing runs many symmetrized variants of each circuit and averages them. Its cloud simulator accepts the option but runs no variants (averaged and sharpened results were identical), so it is emulated here: every two-qubit gate is Pauli-twirled and a random subset of qubits is flipped before measurement and flipped back in the bits. Twirling turns coherent gate errors into random ones; readout flips make readout error the same for 0 and 1. On IonQ each repetition runs 4 random variants of 2048 shots (same 8192 shots). On the Aer backends the chart shows the limit of infinitely many variants, simulated exactly by replacing each two-qubit error with its Pauli twirl and each readout error with its symmetric average; 4-variant runs on Aer are in the table as a check (they agree within shot noise).

Debiasing barely moves anything here. The IBM noise models are already random (depolarizing plus relaxation), so there is nothing coherent to remove. On the honest profile, whose two-qubit gates carry a small coherent ZZ over-rotation, it recovers part of the margin lost to noise (margin vs exact rises by about 5 to 20 percent), but accuracy on 95 pairs does not change measurably: 30 to 45 percent of pairs are within shot noise of a tie, so a few points of accuracy is noise.

When the output distribution stops changing

  • Effective outcomes K: (Σ√p)², the number of equally likely outcomes with the same spread.
  • Shots to 1% (0.1%): shots per circuit at which a sample is expected to be within 1% (0.1%) Hellinger infidelity of the backend's own distribution, (K − 1) / 4ε. The fitted column measures the same from two independent halves of the data.
  • Noise bias: infidelity to the exact distribution that remains with infinite shots. Crossover S* = (K − 1) / (4 · bias): beyond it, more shots do not bring the distribution closer to the exact one.

All results

Accuracy is the mean over 5 repetitions; its standard deviation and variance are across repetitions (shot and noise sampling), not across trained seeds. Agreement is the share of pairs decided as in exact simulation. Margin vs exact is the median ratio of the backend's pos − neg margin to the exact one (noise contraction). Below shot noise is the share of pairs whose margin is under 2σ at 8192 shots.

Hardware assumptions

IBM: per shot = scheduled circuit duration + 250 µs repetition delay, about 25 kW for the whole system, pay-as-you-go list price per QPU second. IonQ: Aria-class gate and readout times with 1 ms per-shot overhead, Forte-class error rates, about 10 kW (an assumption, not a published figure), AWS Braket list price per shot. Rough orders of magnitude, for comparing readouts and platforms only.
